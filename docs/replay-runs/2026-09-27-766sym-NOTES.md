## What the runs say

1. **Execute now beats Watch on every bracket cell.** Since B1 (Gann's own swing charts) the
   Execute bucket is positive under both entry rules, and above Watch in all four bracket
   cells. That is the first time on a production-faithful replay. The 6/3.5 cutoffs hold on
   this evidence. There is no per-score-band split in the report to move them with, and the
   Execute samples (38–60) are too small to justify moving them either way.
2. **The confirmed entry still wins.** +0.190R overall in both runs against −0.052/−0.070 for
   the stop entry. This is the evidence behind C5 (confirmation on every path, automation
   included).
3. **Run 11's Execute bucket is smaller and weaker than run 8's** (60 → 38/44 trades,
   +0.434 → +0.350 confirmed). Run 11 changed several things at once (the Stage A/B2 trend,
   time and level infusion, and the breakaway hold), so the drop can't be pinned on the
   breakaway rule alone. A later run should isolate it.
4. **Gann's exits: better across the population, worse on Execute, and not yet a like-for-like
   result.**
   - With no fixed target, the gain comes almost entirely from trades still open at the
     replay's 10-session limit (`timeout`: +3.9R and +5.7R average, 59 and 80 trades). Those
     are marked to market, not closed by any rule, so they overstate what a finished trade
     would realise.
   - Stops at the initial level lose −1.1 to −1.2R, break-even exits land near 0, the hold
     test cuts losers (−0.7 to −1.5R), and the final-stage trail is small but strongly
     positive (n 2 and 9).
   - On Execute, Gann exits did worse than the bracket (−0.011 vs +0.350 confirmed; +0.099 vs
     +0.220 stop).
   - The report's win rate and profit factor are meaningless for the Gann cells: they count
     only `win`/`loss` outcomes, and most Gann-exit trades end as `timeout` or `break_even`.
     Read expectancy only.

## Decision taken from this

The live exit manager stays on the bracket for now. The switch was conditional on the replay
agreeing, and it doesn't yet: the Execute bucket, the one Guided Mode and automation actually
trade, is worse under Gann's exits, and the population-wide gain rests on marked-to-market
open trades. The next measurement needs a longer holding horizon (so trades close on a rule
rather than a timeout) before this is decided.

### What the runs say

1. **The intraday question is answered, and the answer is no.** Traded on one-hour bars over six
   years, every cell loses money and every interval sits wholly below zero, in both halves. The
   Execute bucket is *worse* than Watch in all three cells. This is the largest sample GSPS has
   measured (8,600 to 17,000 trades against 350 to 600 on the 15-minute runs).
2. **The 15-minute runs only cover two months** (the data the free feed returns at that timeframe),
   so their positive numbers describe one market phase. Run 12 is the first run that spans several
   market regimes, and it does not reproduce them. Until a 15-minute or daily run over years agrees
   with the two-month result, the positive 15-minute figures should be read as a
   single-phase result, not as the method's edge.
3. **The confirmed entry still loses less than the stop entry across all trades** on the six-year
   run (−0.057 vs −0.069), but not on Execute (−0.123 vs −0.090).
4. **Gann's exits, full position, on the longer hold:** better across all trades (+0.259 vs
   +0.198) but worse on Execute again (−0.016 vs +0.306), and 45 of 352 trades still reached the
   time limit (+4.7R average, marked to market). On six years of one-hour bars they are the worst
   cell (−0.074; Execute −0.164). The trend-change exit (+3.1R, n 300), the final-stage trail
   (+1.6R, n 509) and the last-reaction stop (+1.3R) earn; the hold test (−0.51R, n 3,367) and
   three adverse closes (−0.45R) cost, and the hold test fires most often.
5. **What went live is not what was measured here.** The owner switched live and paper exits to
   Gann's rules on 2026-09-28 with 60% still taken at TP1 (`exitRule: "gann-runner"`). None of
   these runs measures that rule. The next full run must include it.

### Decisions this leaves for the project owner

- Whether the six-year one-hour result changes anything that ships. It is the strongest evidence
  so far that the verdict, as currently scored, does not select winning trades across regimes.
  Under "Gann-derived AND measured", that is presumed to be a translation defect on our side,
  and the factor table in the three run-12 reports is where to look first.
- Whether to keep the live exits on `gann-runner` while that is investigated.

### Where to look first (six-year factor tables, inside Execute)

No criterion turns Execute positive on its own; the best cells are still negative. Leads, in the
order they deserve checking as possible translation defects:

- **`historicalSR` inverts on the stop entry** (passing −0.177 vs failing −0.018, n 2,049). A
  setup near a historical support/resistance level does worse, not better.
- **`ruleOfThree` and `swingChartTrend` invert on the confirmed entry** (both −0.155 passing vs
  −0.009 failing). With confirmation, a trade that agrees with the trend enters later and does
  worse, which points at how the two rules combine with the confirmation delay.
- **`volumeClimax`, `gannAngleSlope` and `timePriceSquare` hold their sign** in both cells.
- **Context:** `monthlyBreakAgainstCampaign` (−0.319) and `counterMoveBeyondFirstMonth`
  (−0.215) are strongly negative, as Gann's rules predict (a counter-move into a second month,
  or a break of the prior month's low, is a trend change). Neither acts on the verdict yet.
  `nearMajorPercentLevel`, `counterMoveNormal` and `yearFractionMajor` are the positive leads,
  on small samples.
