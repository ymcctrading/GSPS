## The hexagon plates (Ch. 15B) — findings

**Printed p. 422 (PDF 425): Gann's hand-drawn Hexagon Chart.**
- **Layout.** 1 is at the centre. Ring 1 is drawn inside a small circle: **2 sits directly right of 1**, then 3 (up-right), 4 (up-left), 5 (left), 6 (down-left) and 7 (down-right). The spiral turns **counterclockwise**. Each ring k has 6k cells, and the rows run horizontally.
- **Degree labels.** They run counterclockwise from the lower-right corner, which carries both **0°** and **360°** with an arrow. Then: 30° (right side, lower half), **60° (right corner)**, 90° (right side, upper half), 120° (top-right corner), 150° (top edge), 180° (top-left corner), then the left side (the labels there are faint and read about 210°, 240° and 270°), 300° (bottom-left corner) and 330° (bottom edge). Corners are at 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240° and 300°, and mid-sides at 30°, 90°, 150°, 210°, 270° and 330°. Lines are drawn from the centre to all twelve.
- **Small marks outside the hexagon** (not yet read with certainty): "10 N" beside 60°; "60" beside 30°; "10" beside 0°/360°; "150" beside 120°; "115" and "135" above 150°; "126" beside 90°; "357" beside 300°; "52" beside the upper-left side; "KE" at the top. To be transcribed with the rest of the plate.
- **Numbers on the lines** (ring k = 1 … 11). Checked against the plate at the centre, every corner and several mid-rings; every number checked matched.

  | Line | Numbers on the plate | Rule |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0° / 360° | 7, 19, 37, 61, 91, 127, 169, 217, 271, 331, 397 (the ring completions; 397 is the corner cell) | 3k² + 3k + 1 |
  | 60° | **2, 9, 22, 41, 66, 97, 134, 177, 226, 281, 342** | 3k² − 2k + 1 |
  | 90° (even rings) | 10, 43, 100, 181, 286 | 3k² − 1.5k + 1 |
  | 120° | 3, 11, 25, 45, 71, 103, 141, 185, 235, 291, 353 | 3k² − k + 1 |
  | 180° | 4, 13, 28, 49, 76, 109, 148, 193, 244, 301, 364 | 3k² + 1 |

- **The run 2, 9, 22, 41, 66, 97, 134** lies on **one straight line**, the line to the corner Gann labels **60°**, with 177, 226, 281 and 342 after it. This matches the January 1931 text: on "a same angle of 90°, or an angle of 60° and 240°". The opposite line, 240°, carries 5, 15, 31, 53 ….
- **Is 66 on 180°?** **Not on this plate.** 66 sits on the 60° line, and the 180° line carries 4, 13, 28, 49, 76 …. Gann's text says 66 "occurs on an angle of 180° on the Hexagon chart". The plate doesn't show that, and nothing on the page explains it. Recorded as an open conflict between Gann's text and his own plate.
- **Is it Gann's own drawing?** It is hand-drawn on graph paper, bears the label "W. D. Gann", and is the plate of the January 1931 lesson in this compilation. That is consistent with Gann's own working plate. The scan alone can't prove whose hand drew it.
- **Against the earlier reconstructions:**
  - **B12 (Hayashi workbook):** a different layout. It puts 2 on its 180° line and the second series drifts in angle, and it copies the typeset p. 423, not this plate.
  - **B15 (Global View Technology):** the same as B12.
  - **`hexagonChart.ts`:** right about the ring completions on one line (the 0°/360° line here), but its "66 on 180°" citation is the text's claim, which the plate contradicts.
  - **The lost-plate exception** in AGENTS.md (orphan audit item 7) rested on the plate being lost. **The plate is here.**

**Printed p. 423 (PDF 426):** typeset, unsigned, no lesson text attached. It is not the same construction as p. 422: triangular cells, and the completions sit along a row beside the axis, not on a ray. Provenance unknown.
