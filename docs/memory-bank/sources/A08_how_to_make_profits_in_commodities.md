## Content read (synthesis, with page references)

### Why commodities; the foundation (front matter, Ch. I, pp. 1–29)
- 18 reasons commodities beat stocks: seasonal trend, fewer charts, no cross-currents, crops recur every year.
- The five qualifications (knowledge, patience, nerve, health, capital) and "never buck the trend."
- **Series-of-losses rule.** After 1–3 losing trades "something is wrong with you and not with the market." Get out, wait and study (p. 12). "When a man's trend changes" (p. 17): after a 2nd or 3rd loss your personal trend has changed, so stop; after 4th/5th losses, rest. **Rule 27: reduce trading after the first loss; never increase.** Capital rule (p. 29): **after 3 consecutive losses, reduce the unit to 10% of *remaining* capital.**
- **Wars and booms every 20–25 years** because a new, inexperienced generation arrives. History repeats because human nature does not change (p. 15).
- **Popular trading prices:** the public thinks in 5s and 10s (25, 50, 75, 100, 150), so markets turn just short of round numbers. Don't place limit orders; trade at the market (p. 19).
- News is discounted. When the market *fails* to rise on good news (or fall on bad), something is wrong (p. 8). "Coming events cast their shadows before" (p. 21).
- Normal reactions grow with price: 5–6¢, then 10–12¢, then 15–20¢. Over 15–20¢ means the market is turning abnormal (p. 18).
- Creeping markets end in a "grand rush" (p. 19). Reactions are smaller at high levels and rallies smaller at low levels (p. 23). Strongest position: the 3rd/4th higher bottom (p. 25).

### Chart construction and levels (Ch. II, pp. 29–55)
- **Capital:** $3,000 per 10,000 bu; stop ≤ 3¢; risk ≤ 10% per trade.
- **Charts:** weekly and monthly for the busy trader. **Quarterly (seasonal) chart**: the first break of a prior quarter's low after a long advance, or the first cross of a prior quarter's high after a long decline, signals a change of main trend. Daily chart only in very active, final-stage markets (pp. 30–31).
- **Percentages of the *low price*** (divide by 8): 12½, 25, 33⅓, 37½, **50**, 62½, 66⅔, **75**, 87½, **100**%. **50% and 100% of a bottom are the two most important** (p. 32). For cotton-type markets, 10% multiples (p. 33).
- **Measuring stick:** after the final bottom, take the % of the first secondary reaction; the first reaction exceeding it signals a trend change (p. 33).
- **Time is still primary:** "the first time the time period exceeds the greatest time period" of prior counter-moves, the trend has changed (p. 33).
- **Range divisions:** eighths, then thirds. Also divide the **highest selling price** by 8 and by 3 (p. 34). Importance order:
  1. the range between the all-time extreme high and low
  2. divisions of the highest price
  3. each campaign of a year or more
  4. 3rd/4th lower tops
  - The 50% "gravity center" is the balancing point. "You can make a fortune by following this one rule alone" (p. 36).
- **Secondary bottoms and tops:** the 50% point between the first rally's top and the secondary reaction's bottom is strong support (mirror for tops) (p. 35).
- **Resistance points near the same level: add them and divide by 2** (p. 37). **Resistance Level Card:** the more levels near a price, the stronger it is (p. 37).
- **Lost motion averages 1⅞¢; price will go 1⅞¢ past a level but not 3 full cents**, so a 3¢ stop is caught least often. The gravity-center analogy (p. 38).
- **Nine buying points and nine selling points** (pp. 39–41). These include:
  - old tops and bottoms (the 4th test is dangerous)
  - crossing a series of weekly tops
  - a secondary reaction after the advance **exceeds the greatest rally of the preceding decline**
  - the first rally **exceeding in time** the greatest rally of the prior bear campaign
  - breakaway points
  - 50% of the highest price or of the range
  - double/triple bottoms
  - 2-day reactions in final fast stages, following the previous day's low with a stop
- **Tops and bottoms are swing extremes**, not arbitrary bar highs and lows (p. 41).
- **Triple tops/bottoms years apart are the most important.** A **4th test** usually goes through; if it holds, a big reversal follows (p. 43).
- **28 Valuable Rules** (p. 43): the 24 stock rules adapted, plus #25 "let the market prove it is top/bottom," #26 on advice, **#27 reduce after the first loss**, #28 double mistakes.
- **Formations** (pp. 48–50): single, **U** (flat, 3–10 weeks), **W** (double), **WV** (triple), **WW** (4-bottom); **A** (sharp top after 17–26 weeks), **U** top, **M**, **MA**, **MM**. **Breakaway points** are where the fast move starts after time has run out.
- **Sections:** bull and bear campaigns run 3–4 sections (up to 7 in extreme war years); minor campaigns under 1 year run 2 (p. 50).
- **Space and time rules for change of main trend** (p. 51):
  - after 3–4 sections, a decline exceeding the greatest prior reaction (in cents) signals a change
  - a reaction exceeding the greatest prior reaction **in time** (e.g. more than 4 weeks) signals a change
  - the signal matters more after the 3rd/4th section than after the 2nd
- **Many weeks in a narrow range:** 2–6 or 10–13 weeks. The longer the range, the bigger the breakout.
- **Range of bottoms/tops:** 1–3¢ is a double/triple; 3 full cents through signals continuation. After crossing old tops, 1–2¢ back under is normal and 5¢ is a reversal (p. 53). Moves are faster at high prices (public trades low prices; professionals above $2) (p. 54).

### Forecasting (Ch. III, pp. 56–64)
- **"TIME… will overbalance both Space and Volume. When TIME is up, space movement will start and large volume will begin."** (p. 56)
- **Yearly:** higher bottoms for 5 years, then a lower one, signals a reversal. **Monthly:** add 3 months, then 4 (7), to a bottom for minor bottoms. In bull markets reactions rarely last past 2 months ("2 down, 3rd up"). Grains rarely rally more than 2 months in a bear market.
- **Weekly:** bull-market counter-moves run 2–3 (sometimes 4) weeks and turn mid-3rd week. Fast moves run 6–7 weeks, extreme ones 13–15 weeks (¼ year).
- **Day counts:**
  - **7 × 7 = 49 days: watch days 49–52**, and 42–45 (45 = ⅛ year)
  - **90–98 and 120–135 days**
  - **30, 60, 90, 120, 180 (most important), 270 and 330 days** from tops and bottoms
  - **monthly change days: 6–7, 9–10, 14–15, 19–20, 23–24, 29–31**
- **January rule:** the high and low of Jan 2–7 and 15–21 hold for months. Early-January lows often aren't broken until Jul/Aug or all year (p. 58).
- **Anniversary dates:** 1, 2, 3 years from extreme highs and lows. Within a year watch months 3–4, 6–7, 10–11. Most important at **12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48 months** (p. 58).
- **Year divisions:** ½ = 26 weeks, ¼ = 13 weeks, ⅓ = 17⅓ weeks, ⅛ = 45 days / ~6–7 weeks, **1/16 = 22½ days ≈ 3 weeks** (why 3-week moves are common). A 4th consecutive higher weekly close means continuation. The "5th… of Ascension" is Gann's numerological phrasing, **not admissible**.
- **Minor trend change:** 2+ weeks of tops at the same level, then a break under the 2-week bottom. Dull narrow markets precede breakouts.
- **Bull reactions** last 3–4 weeks and close higher in the 4th; extreme ones 6–7 weeks. **Bear rallies** last 2–4 weeks; one holding into the 5th week likely runs 6–7.
- **Signal (reversal) day, p. 60:** after a big advance, a day that opens high, makes a new high, then breaks under the open and **closes near the low**. Same on the weekly bar. Wider range plus an extreme close means a stronger signal.
- **Weekly open/close rule (p. 60):** at an old top or late section, a week that doesn't trade more than ~1 point above Monday's open and **closes near the week's low** is the first sign of change (mirror at bottoms). The weekly chart with open and close is "one of the best."
- **"Wait to sell until it boils"** (p. 61): bull campaigns end in a fast 6–7 (up to 10) week advance on huge volume. A 6–7 week panicky decline on heavy volume ends bear campaigns.
- **New territory** (p. 62): the longer the time before breaking into new ground, the bigger the move ("accumulated energy"; the dam analogy). Watch 7, 10–12, 25–36, 40–45¢ into new ground. In the 3rd/4th section, new highs don't run far.
- **Volume, four rules (p. 63):**
  1. large volume marks the end of a bull campaign; a secondary rally on falling volume confirms the top
  2. after a 2nd lower top and a dull range, a breakout on rising volume means a further decline
  3. **at bottoms, volume decreases and the range narrows; that is liquidation running its course**
  4. a secondary reaction on falling volume, then an advance on rising volume, means higher
  - **Summary: "Sales increase near the top and decrease near the bottom, except in abnormal markets like Oct–Nov 1929"** (panic bottoms on heavy volume).
- **Open interest** (p. 64): peak open interest before the price high, while volume rises, means the top is near. Rising open interest before the final low means good buying.

## Three-question notes
1. **Gann:** Tier A, 1942/1951. The primary source already cited for lost motion, level averaging, boiling point and the quarterly chart. Extends the time-over-price rules with explicit day and week counts.
2. **Dewey:**
   - The day/week counts are rhythmic windows, and the anniversary and monthly dates are recurrent events (C05). They need hit-rate-vs-base-rate validation.
   - The 20–25-year war/boom generation cycle is a stated cause (endogenous, in Dewey's terms) with repetition asserted, not measured.
   - "Accumulated energy" (a longer range means a bigger move) is testable on GSPS data.
3. **Hermetic:**
   - **Cause and Effect** (time as cause, volume as driving power, accumulated energy)
   - **Rhythm** (sections; seasonal and anniversary returns)
   - **Polarity** (every buying point has its selling mirror; tops become bottoms)
   - **Mentalism** (human nature, hope and fear, generational psychology)
   - Correspondence is weaker here (the same rules across commodities). Vibration and Gender are absent.
