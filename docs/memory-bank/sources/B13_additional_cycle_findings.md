## Content
- **Kitchin** (1923): a **40-month** cycle in US and UK data 1890–1922. W. L. Crum found 39–41 months in commercial-paper rates. **Richard Mogey (FSC): 40.68 months across the entire history of US stock prices.** The Dewey anecdote: 1912 investors reportedly traded a **41-month** stock cycle (Rothschild Consols legend).
  - ⇒ Compare Gann's campaign statistics (Master Course Ch. 10B): top-to-top most often **35–49 months**, and "more bottoms 3–4 years apart than any other." **The two independent sources agree on roughly a 3½-year stock rhythm.**
- **Juglar:** 9–11-year boom/bust waves with four phases (prosperity, crisis, liquidation, recession). ⇒ Gann's **10-year cycle** as the "important cycle for forecasting" (Ch. 10A) sits in this band.
- **Kondratieff:** a 45–60-year long wave. Van Gelderen 1913; Beveridge's wheat cycles average **54 years**; Dewey used **53.25 = 3 × 17.75**.
  - Ray Tomes's essay "Cause of the Kondratieff Cycle": estimated period **53.38 years** from harmonics theory.
  - Proposed cause: **demographics amplified by capital formation** (birth-rate troughs in depressions lead to fewer 15–25-year-olds 20 years later, then prosperity, then higher births, then the reverse). Tomes forecast the best conditions around **2014** and the next trough around **2040**.
  - ⇒ Gann's **60-year "Great Cycle – Master Time Period"** (1935) and the **56¾-year** Square-of-144 cycle (1953) both sit in the Kondratieff band.
- **Schumpeter's composite:** Kondratieff (54) = 3 × Kuznets (18) = 6 × Juglar (9), plus Kitchin (≈ 4). ⇒ An **integer-ratio nesting of cycles** (Tomes's harmonics thesis; see C04).
- **Kuznets:** 16.5–18-year real-estate/demographic cycle. ⇒ Near Gann's **20-year** cycle and Dewey's 17.75.
- **Elliott:** a biographical note only. Excluded under AGENTS.md.
- **Gann section:** biography, plus a verbatim copy of **B09** (see `B_minor_secondary_sources.md` for which of its claims are confirmed and which are not).
- **"Overview of sources for Gann's lessons":** "Gann never published a singular Master Stock Course… he published a string of lessons, many in the form of letters to students, over 1931–1955… the notion of a singular course… has evolved since the 1970s."
  - ⇒ This **explains why the Master Course's chapters carry different dates and inconsistent wording** (e.g. the "Great Cycle" as 20, 60, 56¾ or 90 years). Cite lessons by date.
