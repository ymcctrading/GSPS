## What it claims

1. **"Evidence suggests" Jensen and Gann were colleagues.** No evidence is shown on the pages read, and Jensen's book never names Gann (B16).
2. **The method is on Jensen's p. 111** (Plate 3, U.S. Steel weekly, the square of 90), with detail in the chapters around it. That matches the book.
3. **The key is what Jensen left out.** Gann's "astro-numerological key", based on the Law of Vibration, tunes the geometry to each market and timeframe. It is taught only in the paid course and **not disclosed** on the page.
4. **"Decoded from Jensen and from Gann's section on Descartes in *The Tunnel Thru the Air*":** parallel lines are drawn through future "infinite points", which the article calls **astral points**. It says Jensen showed them as points on a square: fixed mathematical points that exist before and apart from the market data.
5. **Procedure:**
   - When a high or low forms, draw a line from it through the next astral point.
   - Copy the line at the same pitch to the previous opposite pivot (the last pullback before the high, or the last high before the low).
   - Price usually reaches the copy and turns.
   - Where it touches, the point "coincides with" a Law of Vibration price projection.
   - The points take a minute to draw and "are the same every time".

## Checks

- **Jensen's book.** Plate 3 is on p. 111, and it is a weekly U.S. Steel chart in a square of 90 (B16 §3). Confirmed.
  - The construction the article describes is in the book. Lines run from a high or low (the axis) to fixed points on the square's top and right edges (Plate 1, A to G at 5⅝, 11¼, 22½, 30, 45, 60 and 67½ spaces), and the same line is paralleled from the preceding top or bottom.
  - Jensen: "parallel it by the same point line upward from the preceding top"; parallelograms "give clues to when and where a move will be completed" (pp. 112–113).
  - Those edge points are fixed once the pivot and the unit are fixed, and they lie ahead of price, which fits "points in a square" that "exist before the data".
- **Gann.** The same construction is Gann's own (A2.1):
  - the Square of 90 Pattern Chart and its angle table (January 17, 1931)
  - angles from bottoms and tops, including **a 45° drawn down from a low** (his U.S. Steel example: the 1929 low of 150, whose falling 45° caught the November 1930 low of 138)
  - parallel angles from tops and bottoms forming the campaign's channel (GA-12 to GA-16, November 1935)
  - So the falling lines from lows in the article's 2018 chart have a Gann precedent.
- ***The Tunnel Thru the Air.*** The full 423-page text was read for this check (Drive copy). "Descartes" appears only as **Dr. Descartes, a Paris nerve specialist in the novel's plot** (book pp. 244–246), who treats Robert after the ball and tries to have the mysterious letter read. The book has nothing on parallel lines or infinite points. The "decoding" is the authors' own reading of a character's name, not Gann's statement.
- **The "key".** Tuning the unit to each market and timeframe is not in Jensen's book, which fixes 1 point = 1 calendar day ($1 for stocks, 1¢ for grain), nor in any Gann text on file. The article doesn't disclose it.
  - **Tested:** the 2022 lines' slopes, read off the screenshots, were re-expressed in Gann points per calendar day (`pointScale.ts`): about −0.30, −0.56, −0.27 and +0.06. These sit near Gann's 1/3, 1/2, 1/4 and 1/16 ratios.
  - Gann's ratio set is dense (16, 8, 4, 3, 2, 1½, 1⅓, 1, ¾, ⅔, ½, ⅓, ¼, ⅛, 1/16), and readings off a screenshot carry about 10% error, so almost any slope sits near one of them. **This does not identify the key.**
