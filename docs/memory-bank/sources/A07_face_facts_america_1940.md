## What it is
- A political and economic tract written as the Germans broke through in France (May 1940). Most of its length is:
  - a case against the New Deal (debt, taxes, relief, SEC regulation, gold and silver purchases, union labour)
  - a case for aiding the Allies
  - an endorsement of Willkie
- **It has no trading rules.** Its value to GSPS is narrow: how Gann himself describes his time cycles, and a dated forecast whose outcome is known.

## Time-cycle content (the only GSPS-relevant part)
- **Stated basis.** He claims "time cycles… discovered through knowledge gained in reading the Bible." He calls them **"Master Time cycles," "based on the law of mathematics and a repetition of time cycles."** He says he uses them to forecast culminations in the histories of nations and wars "just the same as" tops and bottoms in markets (pp. 1, 12). The method itself is not disclosed.
- **Natural-law premises** (p. 12): "history repeats"; human nature does not change; "action equals reaction in the opposite direction"; "for every effect there must be an adequate cause"; "we reap exactly what we sow."
- **Anchors he counts from** (p. 12):
  - the start of WWI (1914) and its end (**Nov 11, 1918**), which he says he predicted with these cycles
  - the start of WWII (**Sep 1, 1939**)
  - the German drive into Holland (**May 10, 1940**)

  This is the same **anniversary-of-a-pivot** method as the stock books (A04 Ch. IV), applied to historical events.
- **The dated forecast (pp. 12–13):**
  - tide turns against Germany on May 25, 1940, with greater reverses by Aug 10
  - an unfavourable cycle for Germany Jul 10 – Aug 10, 1940, and possible peace by Sep 1 (one year after the start)
  - reverses in Oct–Nov 1940, with **Nov 1–11 = 22 years from the Armistice**
  - Feb 8–15 and Mar 1–10, 1941
  - **May 1–25, 1941, one year from May 10, 1940:** "the end of the war or peace should come by that time at the latest"
  - **Note:** Feb 8–15 sits on the first window of A04's "permanent cycle" (Feb 8–10), and Nov 1–11 near the Nov 8–11 window. This suggests he used the same seasonal calendar here, but he doesn't say so.
- **Longer cycles (pp. 2, 13–14):**
  - **a 100-year repeat**: 1940–50 repeats 1840–50 (low commodity prices, bank failures, depression, relieved by the 1849 gold discovery)
  - **a 20-year generational cycle**: every twenty years a new, hopeful generation "can easily be led"
  - **10 years from 1932** (1942 = another bad year)
  - "the war cycle for the United States ends in 1944 or 1945"
- **A year-by-year outlook to 1950** (p. 14):
  - bad: 1941 spring–summer, 1942, **1943 April–October ("one of the worst periods")**, 1944 and 1947
  - better: **1945 ("a turn for the better… somewhat of a boom" late in the year)**, 1946 (mixed), 1948 and 1949 ("a new discovery or revolutionary invention"; culmination of a boom from 1947–48)
  - 1950 unfavourable in the first half, better in the second half
  - **Deflation, not inflation**, after the war (p. 15).

## How the forecast turned out (checked against the historical record)
- **The war forecasts failed as stated.** The war did not end in 1940 or by May 1941, and it ran until 1945. The dated "reverses for Germany" in mid-1940 coincided instead with the fall of France (June) and the Battle of Britain (July–October). The Battle of Britain can arguably be read as a German reverse in Aug–Oct 1940, but "peace by May 1941 at the latest" was simply wrong.
- **Some of the decade calls fit, loosely.** "The war cycle for the United States ends in 1944 or 1945" and "1945 a turn for the better" match the war's end. The US was at war (1941–45), though his "war *or revolution*" framing hedges both ways.
- **The economic calls largely failed.** He expected post-war deflation, low commodity prices, a US bankruptcy and government bonds at 50. Instead the post-war years brought inflation (1946–48) and a long boom.
- **Why this matters for GSPS.** This is the **clearest out-of-sample test in the whole Gann corpus**: a dated, public, multi-point forecast by his own "Master Time cycles," written before the events.
  - A04's 1929 forecast is a single chosen success, reprinted in his own sales section. This one isn't curated, and **the dated turning-point calls mostly missed.**
  - It is the concrete case for Dewey's *persistence after discovery* criterion: Gann's undisclosed cycle method, applied forward, did not reliably land its dates.
  - It **does not** test the disclosed, rule-based techniques GSPS implements (swing charts, Rule of Three, retracement levels, entry triggers). It tests only the undisclosed "Master Time Factor" forecasting.

## Astrology
- **None named.** There are no planets, aspects or zodiac. The cycles are attributed to the Bible and to mathematics.
- One oddity: a prediction of a sound-"vibration" weapon (p. 14). It is not a market technique.
- For GSPS's astrology track, A07 adds nothing citable.

## Three-question notes
1. **Gann:** Tier A. It is primary evidence of *how Gann framed* his time method (biblical and mathematical "repetition of time cycles", counting from event anniversaries). It is not a disclosure of the method.
2. **Dewey:**
   - *Repetition count*: the 100-year cycle has one repetition, so it fails.
   - *Persistence after discovery*: this forecast is a forward test, and it largely failed.
   - *Regularity of timing* and *cross-series clustering*: untested.
   - This is the corpus's best argument for keeping undisclosed-method cycle claims confluence-only and measured.
3. **Hermetic:** **Cause and Effect** is stated outright and is the tract's organising idea ("deal with causes, not effects"; "we reap what we sow"). **Rhythm** is the premise of the cycles ("history repeats"; the 100-year return). **Polarity** runs through the hope-versus-fear framing (p. 2) and "action equals reaction in the opposite direction" (p. 12).
