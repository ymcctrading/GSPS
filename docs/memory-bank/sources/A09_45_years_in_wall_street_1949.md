## Rules and findings (synthesis, with page references)

### Method premises
- "Human nature does not change… history repeats" (p. 6). Rules must change as times change (p. 6). "Nothing can stop the Trend as long as the Time Cycle shows Up-Trend" (p. 3). Stocks can rise on bad news and fall on good news.
- The three factors are **Time, Price and Volume** (p. 38). Rule 8: **"Time can over balance Price"** (p. 10). "The Time change is more important than reversal in price" (p. 12).
- The averages are used **as a trend indicator for individual stocks** (Rule 1), but Gann rejects **Dow-Theory** rail/industrial confirmation as obsolete (p. 60). Each stock's time period can run out earlier or later than the averages'.

### The 12 trading rules (Ch. II, pp. 6–15)
1. **Trend.** Determine the averages' (or group's) trend with the **3-Day Chart** and the **9-Point Swing Chart**. Trade stocks that conform.
2. **Single, double and triple bottoms/tops.**
   - Old tops become support once crossed, and old bottoms become resistance.
   - **The 4th test of a level usually breaks through** (p. 7).
   - Double-top range on the averages: typically 1–2 points, 3–5 at extremes. On individual stocks: 2–3 points (sometimes 1–2).
   - Stops 1–3 points beyond double and triple tops/bottoms.
   - A triple top or bottom is "often the safest place" because price leaves it fast.
3. **Percentages.** Levels at 3–5%, 10–12%, 20–25%, 33–37%, 45–50%, 62–67%, 72–78% and 85–87%. **50% and 100% are the most important.**
4. **3-week rule.** A ~3-week reaction is the average in a strong bull market (rallies in a bear). After a 30+ day move, watch ~6–7 weeks. After 45–49 days, **60–65 days is about the greatest average bear-market rally or bull-market reaction.**
5. **Sections.** Campaigns run in **3–4 sections**; never call the top (or bottom) on the first section.
6. **Point moves.** Stocks react 5–7 points in strong markets and not as much as 9–10. On the averages, watch 10–12, then **18–21** points.
7. **Volume** with the time periods.
8. **Time periods.**
   - **Seasonal change windows:** Jan 7–10 & 19–24 (most important); Feb 3–10 & 20–25; Mar 20–27; Apr 7–12 & 20–25; May 3–10 & 21–28 (as important as Jan/Feb); Jun 10–15 & 21–27; Jul 7–10 & 21–27; Aug 5–8 & 14–20; **Sep 3–10 & 21–28 (most important, especially for tops)**; Oct 7–14 & 21–30; Nov 5–10 & 20–30; Dec 3–10 & 15–24.
   - **Day counts** from any high or low: **7–12, 18–21, 28–31, 42–49, 57–65, 85–92, 112–120, 150–157, 175–185**. The more important the pivot, the more important the count.
   - **"Market over-balanced":**
     - When a decline's *time* exceeds the prior decline's time, the trend is changing. Likewise when its *points* exceed the prior decline's points (reverse for rallies).
     - Time outranks price.
     - In the 3rd/4th section, smaller gains in less time mean the time cycle is running out.
9. **Higher tops and bottoms.**
   - Check top-to-top, bottom-to-bottom and low-to-high durations.
   - Chart choice: monthly chart for slow, low-priced stocks; weekly when active; daily for high-priced stocks.
   - "The **3-Day Swing Chart is much more important as a Trend Indicator than a daily high and low chart**."
10. **Holidays and trend change.**
    - Change dates around holidays (Jan 3, May 30, Jul 4, after Labor Day, Oct 10–14, election Nov 3–8, Thanksgiving, Dec 24–28).
    - Breaking the last low on the 9-point or 3-day chart is a (temporary) trend change. Crossing the last top is the first signal in a bear market.
    - **Check 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 years, and 15, 22, 34, 42, 48 and 49 months**, from any extreme.
11. **Safest points.**
    - Buy after the bottom, the first rally, and a secondary reaction to a *higher* bottom, when price crosses the first rally's top (stop under the secondary bottom). Mirror this for selling.
    - **2-day reactions** in fast markets show strength.
12. **Speed.** Fast markets move about **1 point per calendar day**. **2+ points a day is abnormal and short-lived.**

### Money and trade management (pp. 16–26)
- The **24 Never-Failing Rules**, the definitive list:
  - capital in 10 parts, **risk ≤ 10% per trade**; stop 3–5 points
  - raise the stop to break-even after a 3-point profit (4–5 on high-priced stocks)
  - trade 4–5 stocks; never average a loss; never hedge; never cancel a stop
  - pyramid only after resistance is crossed; pyramid in small-float stocks, short large-float ones
  - avoid increasing size after a winning streak
- **Pyramid by measured reaction size and time.** Find whether past reactions ran 3, 5, 7, 10 or 12 points, and whether their *duration* ran 2–3 weeks (GM 1924–29). Add on reactions of that size. Widen the stop as profit grows (e.g. by the size of the prior 20-point reaction).
- **Risk/reward:** don't trade for 3–5 points unless the stop is 1–2 points. Profits must exceed losses.
- 25% a year compounded (illustrates realistic expectation). Don't trade sick or depressed ("health is wealth"). Never answer a margin call. No joint accounts. "Hope" is the enemy. Blame yourself, not the market.

### Percentages of high and low *prices* (Ch. IV, pp. 30–38)
- **Ranking:**
  1. **50% of any high or low price**
  2. **100% of the lowest price** (then 200–600%+)
  3. **25%**
  4. **12½%**
  5. **6¼%** (only at very high prices)
  6. **33⅓% and 66⅔%**
- Also compute the **50% point of the range** between extremes.
- A stock breaking 50% of the range, or 50% of its highest price, is weak.
- Worked convergences:
  - **1929 top 386.10** vs 500% of 64 = 384, 1250% of 28.50 = 384.75, and 225% of 119.62 = 388.50
  - **Nov 1929 low 195.35** vs 50% of 386.10 = 193.05
  - **Apr 1930 top 297.25** vs 50% added to 195.35 = 293
  - **1946 top 213.36** = the 50% point of 386.10–40.56 (213.33), and 425% of 40.56 (212.94)
- **Rule 2 as applied (p. 34):** it takes **~5 points beyond a resistance level** to confirm a definite change of trend.

### Short time periods correct prices (Ch. V, pp. 38–56)
- A sharp, fast counter-move corrects the technical position; the main trend then resumes. Keep in mind the greatest time period of counter-moves in the current campaign.
- **Normal swing about 20 points; 10 points is the smallest normal rally** (1932's final leg had none, hence liquidation).
- Recurring durations: **60–72 days**, **90–98 days**, 1 year from the prior extreme, and **49 months** (1938→1942 lows; 1942→1946 top).
- **Secondary rally** (after the first sharp decline from a top) and **secondary decline** (after the first rally from a bottom) always come. They run 154–172 days in the big cases. The advance after a secondary decline is usually faster and larger than the first.
- Round numbers (100, 200, 300, 325) attract public selling.

### Chapter VII — the 3-Day Chart and the 9-Point Swing Chart (pp. 60–81): primary text
- **3-Day Chart construction (p. 62):**
  - When the market makes **higher bottoms and higher tops for 3 consecutive days**, move the line up to the top of the third day, and keep following each higher top.
  - Ignore 2-day counter-moves, except near extremes or in very wide markets.
  - After **3 days of lower bottoms**, move the line down to the low of the third day.
  - **Signal:** breaking the last 3-day bottom means lower; crossing the last 3-day top means higher.
  - Calendar days, **built from highs and lows, not closes.**
- **9-Point Swing Chart (p. 75):** the line follows the move until a **counter-move of 9 points or more** (on the Dow averages) reverses it. Moves under 9 points are recorded only near tops/bottoms.
- **Statistics, 1912–1949:**
  - **464 swings of ≥ 9 points**, about 1 per month; 54 moves under 9
  - **9–21 points: 271, over half**
  - 21–31: 61 (about 1 in 4)
  - 31–51: 36 (about 1 in 8)
  - over 51: only 6, all 1929
- **Watch levels:** a failure to rally 9 points is weakness; a reaction under 9 in an uptrend is strength. A move over 10 tends to reach 20; over 21, watch 30–31. Next are 45 and 50–52.
- Time matters: "the longer the time which has elapsed when a Top is crossed… the greater the advance or decline."

### Months of extremes (Ch. VIII, pp. 82–89)
- **Bull-campaign tops** (35 campaigns, 1881–1949): **September 8**, Jan/Feb/Mar/Apr/Jun/Jul/Oct/Nov 4 each, May and Dec 2, **August 0**.
- **Bear-campaign bottoms** (36): **November 6, July 5**, then April/June/December 4 each; January and September 1.
- **Calendar-year highs** over 69 years: **January 14, December 13, September 10**, November 8. **Calendar-year lows:** **December 13, February 10, January 9**.
- **Time swings** (292, 1912–1949): 3–11 days 41; 11–21 days 65; 22–35 days 65 (so **11–35 days is over ⅓ of all swings**); 36–45 days 31; 43–60 days 33; 61–95 days 20; 96–112 days 13; over 112 days 12.

### Anniversary dates and resistance clusters (Ch. IX, pp. 90–97)
- **Anniversary dates:** stocks change trend in the months, and near the dates, where they made extreme highs and lows. Sep 3 1929 and Jul 8 1932 are checked year by year, 1930–1948.
- **"There is always a mathematical proof of why bottoms and tops occur so many times around the same level":**
  - 127–130: 8 independent percentage/fraction derivations, 11 turns
  - 193–196: 8 derivations, 4 turns
  - 158–163: 10 derivations, 8 turns
- Fourth time at a level: expect a break (p. 97).
- News anchors: the war-start price levels (1914, 1939, Pearl Harbor 1941) become later support and resistance.

### Volume (Ch. X, pp. 98–103)
- **"Large volume always tells of the top."** Volume shrinking to multi-year lows at the bottom (Feb–Apr 1942 at about 8M/month; Feb and Jun 1949) shows liquidation is complete.
- The heaviest volume comes in the last year of a bull campaign.

### Other chapters
- **Ch. XI:** small-float stocks advance more in bull markets (less money to absorb supply).
- **Ch. XII:** puts, calls, warrants (limited-risk leverage; trading against options).
- **Ch. XIII:** inventions end depressions.
- **Ch. XIV:** great operators went broke from **overtrading, greed for power, and not allowing for the unexpected**; those who kept money used stops within about 5 points.
- **Ch. XVI:** the "**Master Time cycle**" used for 30+ years (named, not disclosed) forecasts the post-war boom ending 1948, a depression in 1950–52 and a possible bear-market end Oct–Nov 1952, with dated 1950 windows. These are forecasts, **not rules**, and not testable claims for GSPS.

## Three-question notes
1. **Gann:** Tier A, 1949, his last general stock book. It supersedes earlier lists where they differ (seasonal dates, the 24 rules), so cite A9 with the year. It is the **primary definition of the 3-day and 9-point charts.**
2. **Dewey:**
   - Gann's own swing tallies (464 swings; 292 time swings; month counts over 69 years) are **repetition-count and period-clustering evidence**. They are not dominance, constancy or out-of-sample tests.
   - The seasonal and anniversary dates are **recurrent events** (C05): validate by hit rate against a base rate.
   - The month counts include a calendar-boundary artefact.
   - The "Master Time cycle" forecast is undisclosed, so it can't be evaluated.
3. **Hermetic:**
   - **Rhythm** (secondary rally/decline always follows; sections; seasonal returns)
   - **Polarity** ("reverse the rule"; tops become bottoms)
   - **Cause and Effect** (volume and time as causes; "effects always follow causes," p. 130)
   - **Correspondence** (the same percentage rules for the averages and each stock)
   - **Mentalism** ("human nature does not change"; hope and fear)
   - Vibration and Gender are absent.
