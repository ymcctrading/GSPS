## Content (synthesis)

### Framework: Feynman's order of assumption
Dewey credits Richard Feynman with the Foundation's operating philosophy:
1. **Assume a cycle is chance first.**
2. If it cannot reasonably be chance, assume an **internal (endogenous)** cause within the phenomenon or its system.
3. Only if neither fits, postulate an **external (exogenous)** cause.

Dewey states plainly that the existence of external cyclic forces is **not proved**. They are inferred backwards from behaviour, like a Martian deducing radio stations from two receivers playing the same tune.

### The full checklist: 18 criteria (plus common sense), not 7
**Internal evidence (one series on its own), criteria 1–10:**

| # | Criterion | Dewey's example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dominance**: the cycle prevails over randoms and other cycles. Measure on detrended data; fluctuations as % of trend. | Lynx skins 1735–1963, 9.6 yr |
| 2 | **Regularity of timing**: repeats "with a beat." | Textile sales 1931–40, 3-month cycle |
| 3 | **Number of repetitions**: many irregular repetitions convince as much as a few regular ones. Coin-toss argument. | Canadian Pacific ton-miles, 9.18 months × **48 repetitions** |
| 4 | **Constancy of period** across the record | Lake Saki varves, **17.323 yr** over 4,160 years, fitted in five sections of 48 waves each |
| 5 | **Re-establishment of phase after distortion** | Pig-iron production rate-of-change, **41-month** cycle, 1901–40. It returns to schedule after WWI and after 1932–33. Most crests fall within 3–4 months of ideal. |
| 6 | **Persistence through changed conditions**: "does the cycle control, or do circumstances?" | English wrought-iron prices 1288–1908, **16⅔ yr**, through the Industrial Revolution |
| 7 | **Persistence after discovery**, or when the period is **postulated in advance** from basic cycle knowledge. The out-of-sample argument: with ~50 candidate periods in-sample, confirming one predetermined period out-of-sample cuts the chance explanation ~50-fold. Dewey notes it could be done routinely by fitting on part of the data and projecting onto the rest, but usually isn't. | **Benner's 1874 pig-iron rule** (peaks at 8/9/10-yr intervals, troughs 7/11/9, repeating): trading it 1872–1939 gave a **50:1 gain/loss ratio**. It decayed because the true period is **9.2 yr** and Benner couldn't handle fractional periods, so it slipped a year every five waves. |
| 8 | **Several cycles fitted on part of the data describe later-found data** (complex cycles) | Malleable-iron pipe fittings: 21-, 30-, 41- and 44-month cycles fitted on 1923–27 and 1933–48 **predicted the missing 1927–32 data** |
| 9 | **Wave shape** is non-random: smooth or symmetrical, found by averaging many waves. Asymmetry (fast up, slow down) is allowed. | 42-yr cotton; Cepheid light-curves; the 41-month pig-iron cycle rises fast and falls slowly |
| 10 | **Mathematical tests** | **US stock prices 1834–1966, 9.2-yr cycle**. Bartels test: chance ≤ 1 in 5,000. |

Dewey adds that any one criterion except dominance and wave shape can stand alone, and more criteria mean more confidence.

**Comparative evidence (across series), criteria 11–18:**

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 11 | **Identity of period** across many phenomena, e.g. 30 series at 9.6 / 9⅔ yr. Dewey warns to compute the chance rate of coincidence given how many periods have been alleged. |
| 12 | **Synchrony**: same-period cycles turn at about the same calendar time. 18.2-yr cycles in 16 series cluster at 1962.0 / 1943.8. Tests that assume *identical* turning dates are invalid, because a common force can peak one series and trough another (fur coats vs ice cream). |
| 13 | Synchrony and identity mean more when some members are **significant on their own**. A significant member (8-yr pig iron, 21 repetitions) lends credibility to weak ones (8-yr Goodyear sales, 3½ repetitions). |
| 14 | **Identity of cycle shape** across series: Lake Saki varves vs Nile floods, 17.3 yr, even the five-fold serration. |
| 15 | **Geographical pattern**: "latitudinal passage" (Wing). Crests come later toward the equator, about 70% of the period across 90°. |
| 16 | **Magnetic/landmass distortion** of that pattern |
| 17 | **Period clustering**: 1,404 economic cycle reports (151 series, 327 authors) bunch at ~3½, 6 and 18 yr, not 14, 19 or 21. Dewey's caveat: investigators may have copied each other. |
| 18 | **Families of cycles**: ×2 and ×3 progressions from **17.75 yr**; 18 of 24 lattice periods between 0.22 and 479.3 yr found. Dewey's caveats: with so many alleged cycles, near-matches are easy, and crude measurement means 7½ vs 15 may really be 7.45 vs 15.10. Counter-test: try progressions of 5 or 7; they fit worse. |

Plus "perhaps another: **common sense**. Cycle study is still something of an art."

### Causes
- **Cause of the pattern ≠ cause of each move.** A steel executive "knew the cause" of all 23 turns in the 41-month cycle. What he couldn't explain was why 23 different causes arrived at such regular intervals.
- **Free oscillations (internal):**
  - *dynamic* (a weight on a spring; prices too high → fall → too low → rise)
  - *feedback* (thermostat)
  - *predator-prey*
- **Forced oscillations (external):** day/night, seasons, tides. The forcing period need not equal the response period (tides).
- **Diagnostic.** Only two single-series criteria separate forced from free cycles:
  - **(6) persistence through changed conditions**
  - **(5) phase-resumption after distortion**
  - A stopped pendulum resumes **out of phase**; a forced cycle resumes **in phase**.
  - Comparative criteria 11, 12, 14, 17 and 18 also point to external forcing.
- **Inter-phenomenal clues.** Company B shows an 18¼-yr cycle Company A lacks, which reveals a market A isn't selling to. Identity of rhythm alone "in no way proves an interrelationship."
- Tips for seeing cycles: they are distorted by trend, randoms and other cycles. Crests "almost never come exactly on time" and extra crests appear. **Look for areas of strength followed by areas of weakness.** Consider every point, not just crests.

## Findings for GSPS (important)
1. **AGENTS.md's "Dewey's seven-item checklist" is a subset of Dewey's own 18 criteria.**
   - The seven map to: 1 dominance, 2 regularity, 3 repetitions, 4 constancy, 5 phase-resumption, 9/14 wave shape, and 11/12 cross-series clustering.
   - **Omitted and directly relevant to GSPS:**
     - **#7 persistence after discovery (out-of-sample)**
     - **#6 persistence through changed conditions (regime robustness)**
     - **#10 mathematical tests (significance)**
     - **#18 families of cycles**, which ties to the Tomes/Gann 2×3 lattice
   - Recommend AGENTS.md record the source (C06) and state that the seven are a working subset. When a claim is actually being validated, criteria 6, 7 and 10 should be answered too.
   - Criterion 7 is exactly what GSPS's replay/backtest does (`lib/backtest/replay.ts`, attribution). Dewey endorses the **fit-on-part, project-onto-rest** design, which supports walk-forward, pre-registered periods over in-sample searching. `spectralCycle.ts` searches many candidate periods in-sample, so Dewey's 50-fold argument applies: its best period must be treated as unconfirmed until it holds on later data.
2. **Benner's cycle is a cautionary worked example for Gann time counts.** A rule built from integer-year intervals worked for decades, then drifted because the true period was fractional. GSPS's fixed calendar cycles (`timeCycles.ts`) should be anchored and counted in days or bars where Gann gives day counts, not rounded years.
3. **"Crests almost never exactly on time; look for areas of strength then weakness"** supports treating Gann time projections as **windows with tolerance** (consistent with C01's average-vs-specific point). Most of Dewey's crests fall within ±3–4 months on a 41-month cycle, about ±9%.
4. **Gann's "cycles repeat because human nature does not change" (A11) is an endogenous (dynamic/feedback) claim** in Dewey's taxonomy. Dewey's diagnostic says such cycles resume after a shock *out of phase*, not in phase. So GSPS should not assume Gann time counts keep their phase across a major market dislocation unless measured. Phase-resumption must be tested (criterion 5), not presumed. **This is a nuance for the report, not a rule change.**
5. **Stock-market periods Dewey reports:** 9.2 yr (1834–1966, Bartels 1/5000), and the 41-month family (pig iron here, stocks in B13/C01/C03). Compare Gann's **10-yr** cycle and 35–49-month campaign spacing.
