## Bottom line

1. **The book makes no link to Gann.** It never names him, cites him or describes working with him. The only connection is the publisher, Library of Gann, the house that reprinted Gann's works.
   - Jensen credits his charting method to his partner **George E. Liggett**, expanded by **Virgil Moore** ("the Moon Man" of the grain markets).
   - Whether Jensen was Gann's colleague can't be settled from this book. The article may say more.
2. **His "geometry" is Gann's own Pattern Chart.** Section III (from 1934) is the Square of 90 that Gann taught in his lesson of **January 17, 1931** (A2.1 Ch. 2 and Ch. 4, GA-1 to GA-16). It uses the same angle table (3¾°, 7½°, 15°, 18¾°, 26¼° … 86¼°), the same 8 × 8 squares to the inch, and the same counting of spaces from each major high and low.
   - It adds Jensen's worked examples and a few rules of his own (§3).
   - Since Gann's lesson is on file, this corroborates Gann rather than adding to him.
3. **His astrology is his own method,** and it never refers to Gann's. It is a geocentric aspect method on Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and the Moon's North Node, read against the 7°–9° Gemini point.
   - It doesn't decode Gann's 1954 letter or his "active angles".
   - It does give what Gann never wrote down: **a fixed set of aspects and orbs** for reading Jupiter–Saturn and the other slow-planet aspects in markets, used publicly for 40 years. That makes it the natural pre-registered specification for testing Gann's own Jupiter–Saturn aspects (AS3) without searching orbs (§7).

## 1. Section I — *Major Trends in American Economics* (1935)

- **Cause.** Jensen argues that vibration (solar and planetary radiation) moves mass psychology, which moves business statistics.
- **Sunspot cycle.**
  - The cycle runs about 11.2 years and varies by about 4 years. The fall from maximum to minimum averages 6.5 years; the rise averages 4.6.
  - The book includes a table of yearly sunspot averages for 1837–1934.
  - The solar "constant" reads about 1.92 at sunspot minimum and 1.96 at maximum.
  - Jensen's 1935 projection: a high in 1938, then a low in 1943–44.
- **Planetary periods** (Jensen's figures): Mercury 88 days, Venus 224, Mars 1 year 322 days, Jupiter 11 years 314 days, Saturn 29 years 167 days, Uranus 84 years, Neptune 164 years 167 days, Pluto about 330 years (a 1936 estimate; the true figure is about 248).
- **Slow-planet aspects and the business cycle.** When Saturn, Uranus and Jupiter are 90° or 180° apart, depression follows. At 60° or 120°, prosperity follows.
- **Jupiter–Saturn conjunctions, about every 20 years.** From 1840 they fell in earth signs (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn), with credit and currency upheavals following. The list of presidents elected in those years who died in office also appears here.
- **The Uranus cycle (84 years) and 8°–9° Gemini, Jensen's "American" point.**
  - Uranus stood at 8°–9° Gemini in 1523 (first Spanish grant), 1607 (Jamestown), 1691 ("the Revolution") and 1775–76.
  - It stood at **8°51′ Gemini at 12:20 p.m. on July 4, 1776**, and again in 1859–61 (secession, Fort Sumter).
  - Jensen projected the next return for 1943–44.

## 2. Section II — astro-economic interpretation (1936, with 1977–78 additions)

- **Zodiac.** Tropical, measured from the vernal equinox ("the intellectual zodiac"), and **geocentric**. Jensen rejects heliocentric positions as distorted from the Earth's standpoint.
- **Aspects:**
  - **Square family** (activating, "unfavourable", generally bearish): 22½°, 45°, 90°, 135° and 180°.
  - **Trine family** (harmonising, generally bullish): 15°, 30°, 60°, 120° and 150°.
  - **The conjunction** takes the nature of the planets in it.
  - **Quintile family** (the "triggers" that time trend reversals): 18°, 36°, 54°, 72° and 144°.
  - **Two numeric series** (p. 135):
    - 9, 18, 72, 90, 162, 180: peaks of intensity between major planets
    - 27, 54, 126, 180: "market trend low points often appear at these angles"
- **Orbs.**
  - About 5% each side of the aspect, so 90° ± 4½°.
  - The conjunction and opposition get ±9°.
  - The quintile family gets ±2°.
  - Elsewhere he calls 2–5% generous and adds that a **trigger** is needed: a fast planet (Mars, sometimes Mercury) coming into the aspect.
- **What moves stocks: the angles between Jupiter, Saturn and Uranus, plus the Node.**
  - Since 1896, 13 Saturn–Uranus hard aspects matched 13 declines. Of 9 soft aspects, 7 matched clear gains and 2 mild ones.
  - Jupiter–Uranus soft aspects came 28 times, and every one matched gains.
  - The July 8, 1932 low came a day before an exact Jupiter–Uranus trine.
- **Jupiter–Saturn angles read in his worked charts** (1942–1974):
  - bearish at 18°, 72°, 90° and 180°
  - bullish at 26°–27° and 150°
  - 60° (sextile) confirmed a low
  - 126° and 162° marked turning points
- **The Moon's North Node against 9° Gemini.** The Node regresses and takes about 18 years, 7 months to circle.
  - Squares to 9° Gemini (Node at 9° Virgo or 9° Pisces) "approximate major lows": May 1942, early 1961, April 1970, 1979.
  - Lows after a trine differ from lows after a sextile.
  - Jensen gives England's point as 14° Gemini.
- **Other timing keys:**
  - **Sign changes.** A planet changing sign coincides with a change of trend; Mercury changing sign is a strong market factor.
  - **Retrograde.** A retrograde planet has reduced potency.
  - **Timing shifts.** Declination (the obliquity of the ecliptic) can move an aspect's effect ahead of or behind the exact date: by months for slow planets, days for fast ones.
  - **Commodity key days.** New and Full Moon, and the Moon square Mercury, Venus or Mars. A high or low made on one key day holds the trend until the next.
  - **Planetary hours** apply within the day.
- **Conjunction periods** (geocentric):
  - Jupiter–Saturn about 20 years
  - Saturn–Uranus 44+
  - Jupiter–Uranus 13+
  - Jupiter–Neptune 12+
  - Jupiter–Pluto 12½
  - Saturn–Pluto 33½
  - Saturn–Neptune 36
  - Uranus–Pluto 127
  - Uranus–Neptune 171
  - Neptune–Pluto 492
- **Out of GSPS's scope.** Mundane houses and rulerships, a geodetic zodiac anchored on the Great Pyramid (0° Taurus at 31°07½′ E), the "radix" (primary-directions) progressions, solar returns and diurnal charts, Sepharial's eclipse-duration table, weather and earthquake forecasting, and the "points" of wheat, barley, sugar, rye and cotton (which he calls unclear in basis).
- **Base charts.** These include the **New York Stock Exchange, May 17, 1792, 8:52 a.m. local time**. That is the same date Gann uses as the origin of his NYSE Square of 20 (A2.1 Ch. 15A).

## 4. Section IV — "Putting It Together" (1977–78)

- **Trend structure.** An intermediate trend has five phases: three with the trend and two corrections. The third is the largest.
- **Angles as velocity.** On the 1:1 chart, a 15° channel gives resistances at the breakout plus 3¾, 7½ and 15. A 45° breakout gives the breakout plus 5⅝, 11¼, 22½ and 45 (worked from a breakout at 20).
- **Projection (Jensen's own).** Draw a 60° line from the lowest-close day (or week) and a 45° line from the previous low-close day; where they cross estimates exhaustion in time and price. For very fast moves use 60° and 75°.
  - Dow 1960, daily: a projected base at 600–605 in the first week of May.
  - Dow Transports 1974–75, weekly: a projected close of 166 in the first week of April, then a move to 172 in May. The 172 was exactly 45 points above the October 1974 low close.
- **Channel (Jensen's own).** Draw two parallel 30° lines with a midline: ranges below the midline are weak, on it normal, above it excessive.
- **Rhythms.** Price surges of 11 calendar days (9 market days), tied to Mercury's 88-day cycle. The Moon makes 13 revolutions in 364 days, and 7 in 91.
- **Annual forecasts.** Projections were published each December. For 1974: firm early, a peak in late May, a low in September, then a classic low in December. For 1975: a peak on May 29.

## 5. Inconsistencies in the book

- **The Node opposite 9° Gemini.** It is called "a market top index" in May 1956 but is part of a "classic low" in December 1974. As a fixed rule it is ambiguous.
- **Jupiter's period** is given as 11 years 314 days (p. 10, correct) and 11 years 322 days (p. 57, a copy of the Mars line).
- **Plate 1's angle table** prints 54½° for 52½° (§3).
- **Pluto's period** of about 330 years is the 1936 estimate.
