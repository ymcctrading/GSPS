# Gann historical source materials — memory bank

Twenty Gann-related documents have now been reviewed across two 2026-09-15
research sessions: ten books W.D. Gann himself wrote or coded fiction he
wrote (nine read in full or substantially, one still blocked — see A9), and
nine third-party interpretive, promotional, or tangentially-related works.
Indexed here so a future session can work from this summary instead of
re-uploading source PDFs. Public-domain primary texts (pre-1931 US
publication) are quoted moderately where precision matters; everything
still under active copyright (1936 onward, and all nine secondary sources)
is paraphrased throughout, with only short, load-bearing phrases quoted
directly — never full paragraphs, never committed to this repo verbatim.

Organizing note: the seven Hermetic principles (Mentalism, Correspondence,
Vibration, Polarity, Rhythm, Cause and Effect, Gender — the framework
popularized in *The Kybalion* and carried forward in Dennis William Hauck's
alchemical writing) were used as a classification lens for the first five
sources, at the project owner's request, because Gann's own vocabulary
("Law of Vibration," cyclic "harmony," universal "causation") is drawn
from the same early-1900s esoteric register as the Hermetic tradition. The
lens organizes; it does not substitute for the evidentiary weight each
source is given, and is dropped below for the primary-book survey in favor
of a more direct chronological/technique-based structure, since with ten
books in hand the more useful organizing question became "what does each
book actually add," not which principle it echoes.

## Part A — Primary sources: the books W.D. Gann wrote himself

Chronological. Each entry: copyright status, what's genuinely new versus
already covered by an earlier book, and any charts/diagrams worth
recreating as originals later.

### A1. "William D. Gann: An Operator..." — *The Ticker and Investment
Digest*, Dec. 1909

Public domain. In repo:
`docs/doctrine/TheTickerandInvestmentDigest_gannmastery.com.pdf`. The
origin-story/credibility piece (R.D. Wyckoff, editor). Introduces the "Law
of Vibration" — each stock has its own individual "rate of vibration,"
explicitly compared to wireless telegraphy. States 10 years of preparatory
study, 9 months at the Astor Library/British Museum studying records to
1820, and a documented track record (286 trades in October 1909, 264
profitable). Affirms "every effect must have an adequate cause" — and
explicitly **withholds** the method: "Mr. Gann has refused to disclose his
method at any price." No Square of 9, no angles, no numerology, no
astrology — a claims-and-credibility piece, not a how-to.

### A2. *Truth of the Stock Tape* (1923)

Public domain (Internet Archive `in.ernet.dli.2015.166248`). Four books in
one, overwhelmingly a disclosed risk-management and tape-reading manual:
12 numbered money-management rules (mandatory stops, never average a
loser, pyramid winners only, never fix a target in advance); the "Seven
Zones of Activity" phase model; "Crossing Old Levels" — buy/sell near old
tops/bottoms with a stop 3 points beyond, a level crossed a *second* time
safer than the first — worked with real 1914–1922 case studies (Republic
Steel, Crucible Steel, U.S. Steel, Wabash Pfd A, United Retail); the
flat-top/sharp-top "Habits of Stocks" distinction; commodities rules
transplanted from the same logic. Explicitly withholds "the Time factor."

### A3. *The Tunnel Thru the Air, or Looking Back From 1940* (1927)

Public domain (Project Gutenberg #74988). His coded novel — the single
richest primary source found. Chapters VII–VIII drop the fiction entirely
and speak in first person, dated "January 28, 1927":

- **Method, stated directly**: "In making my predictions I use geometry
  and mathematics, just as the astronomer does, based on immutable laws."
  The Bible is named as the literal source of all science ("I hold that
  the Bible contains the key to the process by which man may know all
  there is to know of the future"), with Matthew 7:7 cited as
  method-instruction and Ecclesiastes 1:9 as the warrant for "history
  repeats."
- **"Harmonic analysis" named directly, in his own voice**, as the
  operative technique: "mathematical science... furnishes unmistakable
  proof of history repeating itself and shows that the cycle theory, or
  harmonic analysis, is the only thing that we can rely upon to ascertain
  the future." This is the primary-source confirmation Awodele's 2013 book
  (A-entry B1 below) argued for from a side character's dialogue — here
  it's Gann himself, unambiguously.
- **Vibration, named explicitly**: "The great law of vibration is based on
  like producing like... Wireless telegraphy, the phonograph and the radio
  are based on this law."
- **Explicit, capitalized refusal to explain the mechanism**: "I have
  always looked for causes and when once I determine a cause I can always
  be sure of the effect... IT IS NOT MY AIM TO EXPLAIN THE CAUSE OF
  CYCLES. The general public is not yet ready for it." The clearest
  primary-source statement of the withholding pattern found across every
  source in this research.
- **Astrology, extensive and explicit** — not veiled at all here. Robert
  Gordon studies zodiac signs mapped onto Biblical tribes, then a
  professional astrologer is consulted by letter and gives a full
  technical reading (planetary rulerships, afflictions, a Mars-Venus
  conjunction as a plot trigger); Gann later reasserts the Gemini/Mercury
  framing as "the Ascendant Sign of the United States."
- **Numerology**: letter-cycle numerology used for a detailed, dated 1928
  presidential-election forecast (candidate surnames must start with
  specific letters per a claimed repeating cycle); a Daniel/Ezekiel
  time-unit system ("a time" = 360 days/years/degrees, later reinterpreted
  as 20 years) laid directly onto Biblical prophecy to date WWII's end.
- **Concrete trading technique with real numbers**: a full 1927 cotton
  campaign ledger (dated round-trip trades keyed to government crop
  reports); an explicit pyramiding methodology in "Major Motors" stock
  (front-load size, add on advances, trail stops) with a stated
  money-management rule — never risk more than 10% of accumulated profits
  on a new position, sized so ten consecutive losses would be needed to
  wipe out capital; an explicit "never trade multiple markets at once"
  specialization rule (diversification blamed for the real-world ruin of
  Drew, Sully, Lawson, Livermore, Ryan, and Durant, all named); a stock's
  own age/founding-anniversary used as a timing trigger.
- **Dated predictions**: none of the fictional 1927→1932 war/technology
  timeline lands on its stated dates in detail (a UN-scale US invasion and
  war climaxing 1931–32, radium weapons, a "Tunnel Thru the Air" vacuum
  weapon) — though the mode call (a future war decided by air power) is
  often cited as directionally early and correct.
- **Diagram candidates**: a 100-year paired technology-cycle timeline
  (Ch. VIII — steamship→airship, rail→auto, each pair 100 years apart);
  Ezekiel's "wheel within a wheel" built literally into an aircraft design
  (Ch. XIX).

### A4. *Wall Street Stock Selector* (1930)

Entered US public domain **January 1, 2026** (95-year rule). His own
"sequel," explicitly meant to "bring Truth of the Stock Tape up to date."
Genuinely new content:

- **24 "Never-failing" Rules** (Ch. II) — an expansion of 1923's twelve,
  adding joint-account handling, odd-lot rounding, even-figure price
  avoidance, and personal-luck/trend tracking.
- **The "Rule of Three"** (his own name; he says traders paid him $1,000
  for it): a stock in a confirmed uptrend will never close three
  consecutive days lower without signaling at least a temporary reversal;
  the downtrend mirror uses two consecutive higher closes. Worked in full
  against U.S. Steel's actual 1929 daily closes.
- **"Boiling point" rule**: final blow-off advances/declines in active
  stocks typically exhaust in 6–7 weeks (rarely past 10).
- **Even-figure order placement**: place orders ½–¾ point off round
  numbers, since public/stop orders cluster exactly there.
- **A fixed annual calendar cycle** of specific dates (early
  February/March/May/June/August/September/November/December windows)
  called "a permanent cycle which does not change" for watching
  trend-change regardless of year — distinct from anniversary-of-a-pivot
  cycles, this one repeats every calendar year on its own.
- **Explicit chart-timeframe hierarchy** with a stated "power ratio"
  (weekly ≈ 7× daily, monthly ≈ 30× daily, yearly ≈ 365× daily) and the
  claim that longer accumulation always produces bigger subsequent moves.
- **The 1929 crash, claimed as an advance call**: reprints his actual 1929
  Annual Stock Forecast (sent to subscribers Nov. 1928), which named
  September 3 as the top date and forecast a "Black Friday"/"great
  deluge" — matching the real Sept. 3 top and Nov. 13 bottom closely. Also
  gives his real-time causal read (margin debt, call-money rates, a "grand
  rush too rapid for orderly liquidation") and explicitly distinguishes
  this 1929 "gamblers' panic" from a larger, still-to-come "investors'
  panic" he expected on a ~20-year cycle.
- **"Science"/"natural" vocabulary**, extensively catalogued (relevant to
  a modern researcher's veiled-language theory — see B5 below) — used
  repeatedly to frame his method against "the natural man" who dismisses
  charts as foolishness (a direct 1 Corinthians 2:13–14 citation).
- **19 charts described but lost to this OCR pass**; most of their
  underlying price data survives as raw appendix tables and could be
  reconstructed. Highest-value: the two 1929 forecast "Curves" (his actual
  graphical crash prediction) and a four-panel U.S. Cast Iron Pipe chart
  built specifically to argue "longer accumulation timeframe → bigger
  subsequent move."

### A5. *New Stock Trend Detector* (1936)

**Not** public domain (term runs to ~2031). A genuine bridge book — and an
important negative finding: **zero mentions of Square of Nine, Gann
angles, 45-degree geometry, or "vibration"** anywhere in the readable
text. As of 1936, his publicly disclosed method is still entirely
level-and-volume-and-elapsed-time based. New content: the **"3-point
rule"** (a break of an old top/bottom must clear it by 3 full points to be
trusted, stop placed 3 points beyond — the direct ancestor of 1923's
looser "crossing old levels" idea, now with an explicit numeric buffer); a
3-day confirmation rule; a 2–3 month reaction-duration rule; four
numbered volume-of-sales rules tied to real 1929–1935 NYSE monthly volume
figures; an explicit rejection of Dow Theory as "obsolete"; an
individual-stock-over-averages doctrine (with 1,200+ NYSE stocks, a 20–30
stock average isn't representative); price-band velocity banding (stocks
move faster above $50, faster still above $100); and concrete capital/risk
sizing ($3,000 per 100-share lot, never risk more than 10% of capital,
max 5-point initial risk — the same 10% ceiling that appears independently
in *Tunnel Thru the Air*'s fictional pyramiding rule, suggesting a genuine
long-held personal rule, not a one-off). Also gives a granular, dated
retrospective of the 1929–1932 bear market broken into four "sections"
with exact point-moves and elapsed days. Six named charts described in
detail (Douglas Aircraft, United Fruit, Corn Products, U.S. Smelting,
National Distillers, a United Fruit/Chrysler pairs-trade overlay).

### A6. *How to Make Profits Trading in Puts and Calls* (1937, with a 1941
addendum "How to Sell Puts and Calls")

**Not** public domain. Describes a market structure very different from
modern listed options: bespoke, over-the-counter, dealer-written
contracts, each personally endorsed by a NYSE member firm (no automated
clearing), standard 100-share/30-day terms with fixed schedule-based
premiums (not continuous bid/ask), no secondary market, orders placed by
telegram with cash wired in advance. Extends his money-management rules
to this instrument: buying calls/puts for leverage with capped risk
(worked Chrysler example: ~100% return on a $142.50 call vs. under 20% on
a fully margined stock position for the same dollar move); using puts as
a stop-loss substitute against overnight gap risk; a "trading against the
contract" scalping technique (shorting stock against a still-open call
once price stalls at the strike); rolling/pyramiding monthly instead of a
single stop order; price-band guidance for which stocks to trade options
on. Explicitly cites *Truth of the Stock Tape*, *Wall Street Stock
Selector*, and *New Stock Trend Detector* by name as the source of the
underlying timing rules — this book supplies instrument mechanics and
money management, not new entry logic. **No new geometric or numerological
content anywhere** — confirmed by a full-text search.

### A7. *Face Facts America! Looking Ahead to 1950* (1940)

**Not** public domain. A political pamphlet (~90% New Deal criticism, war
commentary, immigration/tax policy — not market technique), but contains
the clearest primary-source bridge statement found in this research: "By
the use of these Master Time cycles... I can forecast important
culminating periods in the history of countries or wars just the same as
I forecast important tops and bottoms in the stock market." Projects WWII
turning points by anchoring off WWI dates (Aug. 1914 start, Nov. 1918 end)
and adding fixed intervals, and claims a **100-year repeating historical
cycle** (1940s echoing the 1840s). No numerology apparatus, no geometric
content — pure narrative cyclic-anniversary forecasting, contributing
nothing new to trading technique itself.

### A8. *How to Make Profits Trading in Commodities* (originally 1941;
this scan is a 1951 revision reprinted 1976)

**Not** public domain. His most-cited book — and the source a modern
secondary work (B4 below) is built around. Only the first ~15% was
extractable in this pass (a hard OCR/extraction-pipeline limit on the
large scan; the swing-chart-construction section, all commodity-specific
seasonal chapters, and 32 referenced charts were **not reached** — a real,
flagged gap, not a "nothing there" finding). What was captured, in full:

- **The 28 "Valuable" Rules**, verbatim (capital divided into 10 parts,
  risk ≤1/10 per trade; mandatory stops sized 1–3¢/max 5¢; never average a
  loss; never hedge; reduce size after a loss, never increase; "avoid
  double mistakes" — wrong entry and wrong exit compounded).
- **All 9 numbered Buying Points and 9 Selling Points**, in his own words —
  crossing old tops/bottoms, the "safest" buying point (a secondary
  reaction after crossing prior weekly tops), time-exceeds-prior-rally
  rules, breakaway-point rules, double/triple top-bottom rules, and a
  rapid-advance rule with a 1–2¢ trailing stop.
- **The Resistance Level method**: take a move's high/low range, divide
  by eighths (12.5% steps) and by thirds; the 50% "gravity center" is
  called the single most important level, then 75%. A **"lost motion"**
  concept — price typically overshoots a level by up to ~1⅞¢ but rarely a
  full 3¢ — given as the explicit reason stops are set exactly 3¢ (or 60
  points for cotton) beyond a level.
- **"Sections of a campaign"**: a bull or bear market normally runs 3–4
  legs, with later sections (3rd, 4th) weighted more heavily than the
  2nd for trend-change confirmation.

### A9. *45 Years in Wall Street* (1949)

Not public domain (term to ~2044). His capstone book, read in full once
re-uploaded directly (Google Drive's OCR pipeline couldn't handle the
79-page scan; direct page-image reading worked). Genuinely new content:

- **The "24 Never-Failing Rules,"** printed here for the first time as a
  bound, numbered 24-item list (Chapter II) — a further expansion/
  consolidation of the money-management rules already seen across A2, A4,
  A5, and A8, distilled from all of them plus new experience. Same
  substance as before (10%-of-capital risk ceiling, mandatory stops,
  never average a loss, never hedge, equal distribution across 4–5
  stocks, pyramid only after resistance levels clear) — no material new
  rule, but this is the definitive, most-often-cited version of the list.
- **The 3-Day Chart and 9-Point Swing Chart methods** (Ch. VII) — plot
  only reversals of ≥3 calendar days (with narrow exceptions near
  extremes), or, separately, only reversals of ≥9 points on the DJIA;
  crossing the last 3-day or 9-point top/bottom is the trend signal. This
  is the direct, literal ancestor of GSPS's already-implemented
  `lib/gann/swingChart.ts` ("Gann's 3-day and 9-day swing charts" per its
  own header) — one of the closest 1:1 matches found between any GSPS
  module and something Gann explicitly taught his subscribers.
- **A refined percentage-resistance hierarchy** (Ch. IV), with explicit
  importance ranking: 50% (most important) > 100% > 25% > 12.5% > 6.25%
  (extremes only) > 33⅓%/66⅔% — sharper than A8's looser eighths/thirds
  treatment, with worked examples of multiple resistance levels
  converging on real tops (e.g., the 1929 top landing simultaneously on
  1250% of the 1896 low and 225% of the 1919 high).
- **Duration-based "time square" thresholds** — specific day-count bands
  (7–12, 18–21, 28–31, 42–49, 57–65, 85–92, 112–120, 150–157, 175–185
  days) and month-count bands (1–5 years, or 15/22/34/42/48/49 months)
  checked against elapsed time from a high/low, larger bands read as more
  significant.
- **"Market Over-Balanced"** — a price-based analogue to the
  time-overbalance idea already seen in A5/A8: the first rally or decline
  that exceeds the previous rally/decline *in points* (not just duration)
  signals a trend change.
- **Anniversary Dates**, explicit and worked year-by-year 1929–1949: a
  month in which an extreme high/low occurred becomes a date to watch
  every subsequent year.
- **"4th time at a level" refinement**: the 4th test of the same
  double/triple top or bottom is markedly less safe than the 1st–3rd —
  "it nearly always goes through."
- **A full chapter on options and warrants** (Ch. XII), extending well
  beyond A6's puts/calls treatment into deeply leveraged
  stock-purchase warrants during depressions, with real case studies
  (a $1,000 stake in Tri-Continental warrants said to reach $160,000 over
  4 years).
- **A specific, dated 1950–1953 forecast** (Ch. XVI) — month-by-month,
  sometimes day-window, predicted tops/bottoms for 1950, plus a "Great
  Panic" predicted before 1953 — explicitly attributed to his undisclosed
  "Master Time Cycle," with zero mechanism given, same withholding
  pattern as every other book.
- **A rare personal anecdote** (Ch. XIV): a dated, concrete account of
  trading through the same broker as Jesse Livermore in 1913, losing
  everything when that firm failed, and being repaid; later backing
  Livermore financially in 1934.
- **Astrology: absent, exactly like A11 (1954).** A full read of all 148
  book pages found no mention of planets, zodiac signs, horoscopes, or
  any astrological technique — see the resolved tension below.
- **Diagram candidate**: an unusual "rotated" swing-chart layout used for
  the 3-Day and 9-Point charts — calendar dates run top-to-bottom along
  the left margin instead of left-to-right, price runs left-right instead
  of vertically, with reversals drawn as connected horizontal-then-
  vertical step segments (effectively a point-and-figure chart rotated
  90°). Distinct from any diagram in the other nine books.

**The Mikula astrology tension (see B5) is now resolved, not just
flagged.** Two independent late-career books — this one (1949) and A11
(1954, the same year as Mikula's key "personal soybean letter") — are
both, on a full read, entirely silent on astrology. Mikula's evidence for
growing late-career openness comes from *private letters to individual
paying clients*, never from anything Gann sold to the general public. The
simplest reading that fits all the evidence: the public/private split
present from 1909 onward (disclose risk management and tape-reading
freely, keep the time factor — astrological or otherwise — for private
correspondence and paid courses) held all the way to the end of his
career. Gann's published books never disclose astrology, at any point in
a 45-year career; his private letters, per Mikula, did.

### A10. *The Magic Word* (1950, this scan a 1953 reprint)

**Not** public domain. A devotional Bible concordance, not a market or
even political book — 80%+ of the text is verbatim strings of Bible
verses grouped by topic (Love, Fear, Healing, Money, etc.), framed as a
practical "prosperity gospel" reading centered on chanting the name
"JEHOVAH." Zero market-technique content. Its research value is narrow but
real: documents Gann's personal sacred-number framework in his own words,
outside any market context — 3, 7 ("the sacred number"), 9, 10, 12, 21 —
including a **hexagonal geometric construction** ("take seven pennies...
one circle in the center and six circles around it... draw another
perfect circle around the rim") presented as symbolizing the divine name's
letters, not as a price chart — a real precursor/parallel to the
hexagon/Square-of-9 geometry that shows up in his later course material
(compare A-source B5's "Master Egg Course" hexagon chart). Also states an
explicit "one law across finance/health/spirit" framing: "the law is the
same whether it be the spiritual, physical or financial — you reap just
what you sow."

### A11. *Economic Forecaster* (1954)

**Not** public domain. Despite the cover title, this turns out to be a
thin, 16-page promotional booklet (career-highlights timeline, client
testimonials, course price list — Course No. 1 through a "Great Master
Course" at $200–$5,000), not a technical text. Names five forecasting
"factors" — Time, Price, Volume, Speed, and a stated late-career addition,
**Mass Pressure** (a crowd-psychology curve, "shows when the public
becomes over-optimistic") — but explains none of them mechanically,
reserving actual technique for paying course buyers. Real trade examples
with dates/numbers are given as track-record proof (a April–May 1954
soybean trade, an April 1954 coffee trade), not as worked method
demonstrations. **Notably silent on astrology**: a full-text search found
zero mentions of "astrology," "planet," "longitude," "zodiac," or
"vibration" — negative evidence against the theory (see B5) that Gann grew
more openly astrological in his final years, at least in this specific
1954 publication. Numerology present is framed as Biblical/Masonic (3, 7,
9, 12 tied to his "Master Calculator" device and to his own stated
Masonic membership), not zodiacal.

## Part B — Secondary and interpretive sources

### B1. Awodele, *W.D. Gann: Divination by Mathematics II — Harmonic
Analysis* (2013)

Self-published (BEKH LLC), copyright 2013. A modern researcher's
investigation of the *Tunnel Thru the Air* "harmonic analysis" passage —
now corroborated as Gann's own direct statement, not just a character's
line (see A3). Correctly demonstrates real Fourier/spectral
dominant-cycle analysis on public-domain rainfall data and on stock
prices. Strongest corroboration: a real 1922 newspaper article and a 1926
Gann letter naming Henry Ludwell Moore, Schuster, and Fourier by name as
influences. Final chapters (Pythagorean magic squares, planetary
numerology) are openly speculative, not established.

### B2. Numerology forum fragment ("WD Gann Number Vibrations")

Not a book — a 3-page forum/mailing-list exchange, no author credentials.
Digital-root reduction (casting out nines) applied to the 360° circle and
a price-percentage table; the "144 reduces to 9" claim is true of every
multiple of 9 by construction, not a discovery. Names L. Dow Balliett, a
real early-1900s numerology author, as a plausible source of Gann's
"vibration" vocabulary — the one genuinely useful lead. Lowest reliability
of all sources reviewed.

### B3. WD Gann Inc. company blog ("Mathematical Seer") + "D Levels" table

The blog of the company holding Gann's copyrights/archives — mostly
promotional, but contains a reprinted public-domain 1923 newspaper article
on his letter/name numerology method for election forecasting, and direct
confirmation (from the company holding his actual working papers) that he
used real astrology: his private planetary ephemeris annotated with
"planetary midpoints, declinations, Heliocentric transits, and the Lunar
Nodes." The "D Levels" table is a bare, uncredited spreadsheet that
decodes exactly to the Square of 9 spiral formula already implemented in
`lib/gann/squareOf9.ts`.

### B4. Hima Reddy, *The Trading Methodologies of W.D. Gann* (2013, FT
Press/Pearson)

Actively copyrighted (major publisher). A modern CMT's illustrated
commentary built directly around A8's numbered Buying/Selling Points and
the "eighths retracement" technique, reworked onto contemporary charts
(Intel, Google, Cisco, etc.). Confirms and illustrates several of A8's
rules with modern examples; her account of the buying/selling points was
independently cross-checked against A8's actual text and found accurate
where both cover the same ground (she skipped points 2 and 7, now filled
in from A8 directly). No new primary claims — a teaching aid, not a
source.

### B5. Patrick Mikula, *Gann's Scientific Methods Unveiled: Volume 2*
(1996, revised 2012)

Actively copyrighted. The single most technically detailed secondary
source on Gann's astrology — argues Gann used a real, learnable
price-to-planetary-longitude conversion system (adding multiples of 360°
to a planet's zodiac longitude to get a price level), demonstrated against
Gann's own 1948 soybean chart, a 1929 U.S. Steel "naturally" passage, and
a 1954 personal soybean letter naming specific planetary "active angles."
Describes the **"Circle Chart"**/**"Square Chart"** construction in
concrete mechanical detail — exactly the Square-of-9-style spiral/hexagon
geometry, built here explicitly around planetary longitudes rather than
pure price arithmetic. This book's central thesis (Gann grew more overtly
astrological in his private, later-career material even while staying
coy in his public books) is **now resolved rather than contested**: both
A9 (*45 Years in Wall Street*, 1949) and A11 (*Economic Forecaster*, 1954
— the same year as Mikula's key soybean letter) are, on full reads,
entirely silent on astrology. Mikula's evidence is drawn exclusively from
private letters to individual clients, never from anything sold to the
general public — the public/private split, not a late-career shift
toward openness, best explains all the evidence in hand.

### B6. M.G. Bucholtz, *The Bull, The Bear and The Planets* (2013,
iUniverse)

Actively copyrighted. General financial-astrology practice (lunar phases,
planetary aspects, first-trade horoscopes) applied to modern stocks and
commodities — not Gann-specific. Cites Gann only briefly, as an
inspiration, including the author's own unverified claim that Gann was a
Freemason interested in "sacred math and astrological phenomena."

### B7. Larry Pesavento & Shane Smoleny, *A Trader's Guide to Financial
Astrology* (2015, Wiley)

Actively copyrighted. Confirmed, via full-text search, to have **zero
connection to Gann** — general financial astrology with a statistically-
minded methodology (efficiency tests, control-group comparison,
significance testing), credits Evangeline Adams and Donald Bradley as the
field's early figures, never mentions Gann. Included here only to record
that it was checked and ruled out, not as a Gann source.
