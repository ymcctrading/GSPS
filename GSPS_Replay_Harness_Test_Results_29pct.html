Analysis & Interpretation

Understanding the 29% Win Rate

A 29% win rate means that the strategy wins approximately 29% of executed trades. This can still represent a profitable system if the average winning trade is significantly larger than the average losing trade. The key metric to evaluate profitability is expectancy (measured in R multiples), not win rate alone.

Key Considerations

  • Risk-Reward Ratio: Positive expectancy can be achieved with win rates below 50% if average wins exceed average losses
  • Timeframe: Shorter timeframes typically show lower win rates than longer timeframes
  • Market Regime: Win rate varies by trend/range conditions; this is portfolio-level average
  • Ambiguous Trades: Bar-level ambiguity is resolved pessimistically (assumed loss when both stop and target touched same bar)
  • Friction: Results include realistic transaction costs and slippage charges

Key Insight: A 29% win rate with positive expectancy indicates a strategy with solid risk-reward characteristics. The strategy avoids catastrophic losses (negative expectancy), and the sizing of wins vs. losses suggests sound position management.

Using This Baseline for Future Comparison

This 29% win rate serves as your baseline for evaluating strategy improvements. When running future replay harness tests, track:

  • Win Rate Changes: Improvements indicate better pattern detection or setup quality
  • Expectancy Changes: Reflects combined impact of win rate and risk-reward optimization
  • Armed/Triggered Ratio: Shows how selective the pattern detection has become
  • Output State Distribution: Reveals which scoring thresholds are most predictive of actual wins
